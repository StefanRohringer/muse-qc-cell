How it works

  1. Order: I type or speak an order in plain words (with --voice, Muse Voice Transcribe turns the spoken order into text and I confirm it). The Muse Spark agent decides which tool to call next: scan_table, inspect_and_sort(part_id), sort_in_order(part_ids), write_report or finish (with --plan-once the model picks the order once and the parts run in one go).
  2. Scan: the table camera finds four ArUco markers and maps pixels to table millimeters with a checkerboard calibration. An empty-table reference shows what changed, SAM 3.1 confirms each candidate, and the lever direction is read from the dark slot at one end of the part.
  3. Pick: the arm moves above the part, turns the gripper so the fingers close across the levers, lowers, closes until the touch sensors report contact on both sides, and lifts.
  4. Inspect: the arm carries the part to a fixed inspection station. The arm camera takes a close-up, SAM 3.1 finds the part, the crop is enlarged and SAM 3.1 counts the levers. Exactly 3 levers = good. A doubtful count is retried on fresh frames; a part is only good when the count is confirmed.
  5. Sort: the part is dropped into the good or bad box, and the table is scanned again, so a part that moved is found again. A part that appears while planned parts are still waiting joins the running plan; a part that appears after the last planned part is picked up by a new plan. With --arm-session the arm goes from the box straight to the next part and returns to rest once at the end; without it (default) it returns to rest after every part.
  6. Report: every step is saved as JSON, and a live page in the browser shows the table, each part's close-up, its lever count and its box.

Safety

In short: every waypoint is checked before motion, every command is re-checked each control cycle, only a human starts hardware runs, speed is capped, tracking errors abort the move, touch sensing stops the descent, only grasp waypoints may go low, the model passes part IDs only, a spoken order must be confirmed and the yes stays typed, and devices are found by USB ID.

Not certified

muse-qc-cell is a hackathon research prototype. Its safety layers are software checks only. They have not been assessed or certified by any testing body (for example TÜV), and the cell does not comply with the EU Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 or robot safety standards such as ISO 10218. There is no certified emergency stop, no guarding and no risk assessment. Do not use it in production or near people without a proper safety concept, risk assessment and certification.
